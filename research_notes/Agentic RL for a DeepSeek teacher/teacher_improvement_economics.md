# Teacher improvement economics: is improving a DeepSeek V4.1 Flash teacher worth it, or is sampling more enough? (as of 2026-11-03)

Labels. **Measured** = a third party's or this project's own run. **Claimed** = a maker's or vendor's own statement. **Inference** = my reasoning, not a measurement. **(my arithmetic)** = a hand calculation from the cited figures; nothing was run. Prices are list prices read on 2026-11-03 unless another date is given. Dollars and euros are kept apart; no exchange rate is applied.

Shared assumptions behind every cost sum below (all **Inference**). An *attempt* is one agent session on a mined Elixir task with hidden tests. A *short* attempt has ~20k generated tokens (thinking included), ~60k uncached input tokens and ~440k cached input tokens across ~16 turns. A *long* attempt has 40k / 100k / 1.4M. For scale, the project's Flint sessions averaged 13.1k generated tokens over 16 turns ([agentic data pipeline](https://github.com/Cinderella-Man/llms-on-crypto-mining-rigs/blob/v3/docs/research/notes/agentic_data_pipeline.md)). The base pass rate is p0 = 0.30, the low end of the 30–55 % that open models score on the owner's Elixir SWE Bench (this repository's report `reports/Fine tuning small models for Elixir.md`). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 has no Elixir score anywhere. A *kept* session is one that passes the hidden tests.

## 1. pass@k against pass@1: what RL does to coverage, and what it means for a test-filtered data generator

### Takeaway
On math, the evidence is strong that RL with verifiable rewards raises pass@1 and often lowers coverage at large k. A 2026 theory paper argues that under specialisation every pass@1 gain costs coverage on some prompts. Agentic SWE evidence points the other way at the k a data generator actually uses (8–16 attempts): RL on a weak 72B agent raised pass@10 almost as much as pass@1. For a teacher that has already had large-scale RL, the question is untested. By construction, though, a FireFlash-style difficulty filter trains only on tasks the teacher already solves at k = 12 with at least 86 % probability (my arithmetic). So the owner's hypothesis holds in its logic: a test-filtered generator collects pass@k, not pass@1. Its empirical half, that RL adds little to coverage, is supported for strong models at large k and contradicted for weak agents at k ≈ 10.

### Cited Findings
**General RLVR (mostly math):**
- **Measured:** Yue et al. tested six RLVR algorithms across model families on math, code and visual reasoning. RL-trained models win at small k, but base models score higher at large k. The authors say reasoning ability stays bounded by the base model and that the boundary often *narrows* as RLVR continues, while distillation from a teacher can add new reasoning patterns. arXiv v1 is dated 2025-04-18 and v5 2025-11-24; it was a NeurIPS 2025 oral — [arXiv 2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837v4).
- **Measured:** "RL Squeezes, SFT Expands" (Matsutani et al., ICLR 2026) finds that RL cuts incorrect reasoning paths, which lifts pass@1, but also cuts correct ones, which explains why the base catches up at high k. SFT increases the number of distinct correct paths and keeps incorrect ones, which helps pass@k without guaranteeing pass@1. Their reading is that SFT-then-RL combines both effects — [arXiv 2509.21128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21128); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009898). I read this from abstracts and excerpts, not the full paper.
- **Measured (rebuttal):** ProRL (NVIDIA, NeurIPS 2025): prolonged RL with KL control, reference-policy resets and a varied task suite gave a 1.5B model wins over its base across many pass@k settings, including tasks the base never solves at any k. The gains track the base's competence and the training length — [ProRL, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1a22b912945fb7c0bdd079e792b31b6f-Abstract-Conference.html). The evidence is at 1.5B only, and a secondary summary says it has not been independently replicated — [wiki note](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rl-llm-wiki/knowledge-base/discussions/262).
- **Measured (2026):** "When RLVR Shrinks the Reasoning Boundary" (2026-07-12) calls the effect *pass@k inversion*. It finds the loss concentrates on "boundary prompts", where the base has rare correct trajectories that are too sparse to show up in finite rollout groups; in a 3,000-prompt study, plain GRPO lost base-solvable boundary prompts. A per-problem base-anchoring fix improved both pass@1 and high-budget coverage over GRPO on Omni-MATH — [arXiv 2607.20543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20543). The abstract gives no numbers.
- **Claimed (theory, 2026-10-29):** "There Is No Free Lunch in RLVR" models RLVR as an exponential tilt of the base policy. Under exclusive specialisation, any update that raises pass@1 lowers success on some prompt. Removing the large-k crossover entirely costs the whole pass@1 gain, and the same identity yields entropy collapse. This is a single-author theory paper with no benchmark numbers in the abstract — [arXiv 2610.39295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39295).
- **Claimed (abstract level, not read in full):** DEEP-GRPO beats both GRPO and the base at every k up to 128 on GSM8K, while plain GRPO falls below the base at k = 64 (86.8 vs 87.7) — [arXiv 2602.14169](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14169). A curriculum-RL paper reports larger pass@256 gains than vanilla RLVR, which sometimes falls below the base — [arXiv 2606.22317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22317).

**Agentic / SWE-specific:**
- **Measured, the most direct agentic evidence:** Golubev et al. trained Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct on SWE-rebench tasks through rejection fine-tuning (RFT) and then two RL stages. SWE-bench Verified pass@1 / pass@10: base 11.4 / 31.0, after RFT 20.5 / 43.0, after RL stage 1 35.7 / 54.6, after RL stage 2 39.0 / 58.4. The gap between pass@10 and pass@1 stayed at about 19–22 points throughout, so RL raised coverage at k = 10 almost as much as pass@1 (+15.4 pass@10 against +18.5 pass@1 from RFT to the end). On SWE-rebench June the two RL stages were flat on pass@10 (53.7 → 53.7). Compute was 16 nodes × 8 H200, with G = 10 rollouts per problem — [arXiv 2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2).
- **Measured:** DeepSWE (Qwen3-32B, 64 H100 × 6 days, ~4,500 R2E-Gym tasks, 200 RL steps): Pass@1 rose from 23 % to 42 % over RL, with Pass@1 42.2 and Pass@16 71.0 for the trained model. No pre-RL Pass@16 is published, so its effect on coverage cannot be read off — [Together AI, 2025-07-02](https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe).
- **Measured:** Agent-RLVR plots pass@k for k = 1 to 32. A guidance-trained model's lead over the unguided one widens as k grows, which the authors read as more diverse sampling. It compares two RL variants, not a model before and after RL — [arXiv 2506.11425](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11425v1).
- **Measured (FireFlash, 2026-11-02):** RL kept tasks whose initial pass rate was 15–85 %, ran 12 attempts per task (32 tasks × 12 = 384 trajectories per step), and dropped zero-signal groups from 48 % to 10.2 %. Entropy fell from about 0.46 to 0.33 in a healthy run. The post reports **pass@1 only**, averaged over 5 trials: Terminal-Bench 4.0 35.8 → 46.2, DeepSWE v1.1 63.4 → 68.8, SWE-Bench Pro Verified 58.2 → 63.3. It makes no claim about pass@k or coverage — [FireFlash](https://agenteconomics.org/posts/agentic-rl-recipe).
- **Measured:** nearly half of FireFlash's never-solved tasks were broken — [FireFlash](https://agenteconomics.org/posts/agentic-rl-recipe).
- **Measured:** test-time-scaling papers show how far a verifier's choice falls short of oracle coverage. SWE-Gym (32B, OpenHands, SWE-bench Verified): Pass@k 20.6 / 37.8 / 42.8 against verifier Best@k 20.6 / 29.8 / 32.0 at k = 1 / 8 / 16 — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2). DeepSWE: hybrid verifier Best@16 59.0 against Pass@16 71.0 — [Together AI](https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe). R2E-Gym: hybrid Best@26 51 % from a Pass@1 of 34.4 % — [arXiv 2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07164).

### Inferences
- **The generator collects pass@k, not best@k.** In test-time scaling, a verifier has to pick one patch, and the 10–12-point gap between Best@16 and Pass@16 above is lost. A data generator with executable hidden tests needs no selection: every passing session is kept, so its useful yield is close to oracle coverage, less the graders' false positives. That is the core of the owner's hypothesis, and it holds.
- **The filter's arithmetic.** FireFlash trains only on tasks with an initial pass rate of 15–85 %. A task at exactly 15 % is solved at least once in 12 attempts with probability 1 − 0.85¹² = 1 − 0.142 = **85.8 %** (my arithmetic), and tasks above 15 % are covered more often. On its own training tasks, then, FireFlash-style RL mostly turns "solved 2 times in 12" into "solved 8 times in 12". For a generator that already samples 12 times per task, that lowers the cost per kept session but adds at most ~14 % coverage on those tasks. Coverage gains can only come from transfer to tasks outside the filter. FireFlash shows transfer in pass@1 (+10.4 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, held out), but not whether that is new coverage or sharpening.
- **Agentic RL from a weak start does expand coverage at k ≈ 10.** In the 72B study, RL added +11.6 pass@10 in stage 1 (43.0 → 54.6), so that run's RL put new tasks within reach of 10 attempts. A math-style crossover would need k well above 10 in that run. That base was a weak agent at 11.4 %, while DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash has already had large-scale RL: the paper reports millions of concurrent sandboxes and more than 40 teacher models for on-policy distillation ([arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1)). Owner-run RL on top of that is closer to the "sharpen a strong model" regime where Yue et al., the inversion paper and the no-free-lunch theory say coverage gains are small or negative.
- **Entropy matters for data diversity.** FireFlash's entropy fell 0.46 → 0.33. Yuan et al. found that the number of *distinct* correct reasoning paths drives rejection-sampling fine-tuning ([arXiv 2308.01825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01825)), and "RL squeezes" says RL removes correct paths. An RL-sharpened teacher therefore probably gives *more* kept sessions per attempt but *less varied* ones per task, which is the wrong direction for training a student on many styles of solution. This is not measured for agent sessions.
- **The one place RL clearly pays a generator is time.** Raising p from 0.30 to 0.45 gives 1.5× more kept sessions per hour. That is worth little where attempts cost cents (the API), but a lot where the teacher is a single stream and wall time is the constraint (dsx at home; section 5).

### Gaps
- No pass@k curve exists for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on any agentic benchmark. Its paper uses N = 8 samples for DeepSWE v1.1 and N = 3 for Terminal-Bench 2.1 but reports pass@1 ([arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1)).
- I found no study that measures pass@k at k ≥ 32, before and after RL, for a strong agentic SWE model. The 72B study stops at k = 10.
- FireFlash's pass@k, or pass@1 on its own excluded tasks (0 % or > 85 %), is not published.
- Full texts of 2607.20543, 2610.39295, 2602.14169 and 2606.22317 were not read, only abstracts.

## 2. Levers that change no weights: their measured size and cost per kept session

### Takeaway
On the same DeepSeek V4.1 Flash checkpoint, the choice of harness moves DeepSWE v1.1 by 8.7 points, and Pi, the harness used by the owner's Elixir bench, sits 8 points below mini-SWE. Reasoning effort moves it by 8.2 points at 2.5× the output tokens. FireFlash measured +4.4 from its harness and −16 % per-trial cost from workflow prompts. All of these cost almost nothing to test. More attempts roughly double coverage from k = 1 to k = 8–16 on agentic SWE (1.2–2.1×), and a test-filtered generator keeps all of it. Context files tend to *hurt* success while raising cost by over 20 %. A claim that developer-written files help by about 4 points circulates only in secondary write-ups and is unverified.

### Cited Findings
**Harness, same checkpoint:**
- **Measured (DeepSeek's own paper, fixed checkpoint, max effort):** DeepSWE v1.1 resolved / Terminal-Bench v2.1 pass@1 by scaffold: Claude Code 69.8 / 88.0; Codex 65.6 / 84.1; OpenCode 65.5 / 85.0; **Pi 66.2 / 86.1**; **mini-SWE 74.2 / 90.3**; DeepSeek harness minimal 72.6 / 90.6, standard 70.5 / 85.8, PTC 67.6 / 85.8. Four Claude Code versions ranged 68.4–69.8 on DeepSWE, a version-to-version noise of about 1.4 points — [arXiv 2609.19969, §5.3.4 and App. B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1). The paper is dated about September 2026, so the checkpoint is presumably an earlier V4.1-Flash than 1017 (**Inference**).
- **Claimed:** DeepSeek reports V4.1-Flash-1017 at DeepSWE v1.1 77.0 with the Mini-SWE-V2 harness and Terminal-Bench 4.0 42.3 with the Claude Code harness — [model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017).
- **Measured:** FireFlash, on GLM-5.3-Flash before any RL: own harness 40.2 % at $0.46 per trial and $1.11 per solve, against the official Claude Code entry at 35.8 %, $0.36 per trial and $1.01 per solve. Context compaction, output redirected to files and background execution cut per-trial cost by about a third; efficient-workflow prompt guidelines saved a further 16 % — [FireFlash](https://agenteconomics.org/posts/agentic-rl-recipe).
- **Measured:** for two frontier models on an 80-task private suite, the paired harness gaps were ±1.25 points and not statistically resolved. A neutral harness cost 1.2–1.6× as much per solved task. 22 of 81 runs cancelled at the time limit had already produced a passing patch — [arXiv 2609.11987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11987).

**Reasoning effort and session length:**
- **Measured:** on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, effort 25 → 100 raises DeepSWE v1.1 from 66.0 to 74.2 and Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 82.4 to 90.6, at about 2.5× the output tokens. Effort 60–80 recovers most of the gain at under half the maximum token budget, and going from 80 to 100 makes agent trajectories 1.6–1.8× longer. The API's low / high / max tiers map to effort 50 / 75 / 100 — [arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1).
- **Measured:** FireFlash's RL length bonus (up to 0.2 of reward) cut tokens per solve to 0.84× with no loss of accuracy — [FireFlash](https://agenteconomics.org/posts/agentic-rl-recipe).
- **Measured:** for SWE agents, adding turns helps most at low turn counts; after that saturates, gains come mainly from parallel rollouts — [SWE-Lego, arXiv 2601.01426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01426).

**Context files:**
- **Measured:** across several agents on SWE-bench tasks and on repositories with committed context files, AGENTS.md-style files tend to *reduce* success compared with no repository context and raise inference cost by over 20 %. Agents follow the files and explore more widely, running more tests and opening more files — [arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988v1); [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10021235).

**More attempts with a verifier (agentic pass@k curves):**
- **Measured:** SWE-Gym 32B: pass@1 / 8 / 16 = 20.6 / 37.8 / 42.8 — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2). DeepSWE: Pass@1 42.2, Pass@16 71.0 — [Together AI](https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe). Qwen2.5-72B after RL: pass@1 39.0, pass@10 58.4 — [arXiv 2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2). Large Language Monkeys: SWE-bench Lite coverage rose from 15.9 % with one sample to 56 % with 250 — [arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787v3). SWE-Master: pass@1 61.4 against pass@8 76.2 on Verified, from a search excerpt — [arXiv 2602.03411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03411v2).
- **Measured:** SWE-Gym's authors say performance was limited by the sampling compute budget, not by the number of tasks — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2).
- **Measured:** SWE-Replay reuses earlier trajectories instead of sampling from scratch, cutting the cost of naive test-time scaling by up to 17.4 % while keeping or improving results by up to 3.8 % — [arXiv 2601.22129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22129v1).

**Privileged hints during data curation:**
- **Measured:** P2T uses the developer's reference patch as privileged information during curation only (the student never sees it). On 1.8k SWE-Gym instances it raised the student's SWE-bench Verified pass@1 by up to 10.8 points and cut per-instance inference cost by about 15 % — [arXiv 2605.21996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21996), read through abstracts only.
- **Measured:** guidance during RL rollouts raised pass@k, and Agent-RLVR took SWE-bench Verified from 9.4 % to 22.4 % with 817 environments — [arXiv 2506.11425](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11425v1).

### Inferences
- **Coverage multipliers (my arithmetic):** coverage at k = 8–16 divided by pass@1 is 37.8/20.6 = 1.83, 42.8/20.6 = 2.08, 71.0/42.2 = 1.68, 58.4/39.0 = 1.50 and 76.2/61.4 = 1.24. The multiplier shrinks as pass@1 rises. At p0 = 0.30 a plausible coverage at k = 8–16 is about 0.45–0.60 of tasks.
- **Harness is the cheapest large lever, with a catch for data.** The owner's bench runs Pi, which scored 66.2 against mini-SWE's 74.2 on the same checkpoint. That is +12 % relative, possibly more on Elixir; Elixir is untested. But sessions teach the student the tool protocol they were recorded in. Generating in mini-SWE, which is bash-only, and deploying the student in Pi is a format mismatch that nobody has measured. The safer use is to generate in the harness the student will run in and copy mini-SWE's habits into it, such as minimal tools and output redirected to files.
- **Cost per kept session, by lever** (my arithmetic, DeepSeek API at off-peak / peak rates, short attempt = $0.022 / $0.045, long = $0.043 / $0.086; section 4 shows the sums). Each lever's effect is carried over from another benchmark, so treat the rows as illustrations:

| Lever | Assumed effect on p and tokens | Cost per kept session (off-peak – peak, short attempt) | Coverage effect |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Base, Pi, effort "high" | p = 0.30 | $0.074 – $0.149 | Baseline |
| Better harness (Pi → mini-SWE-like) | p × 1.12, tokens unchanged | $0.065 – $0.133 | Likely up, also on hard tasks |
| Workflow prompts (−16 % per trial) | p unchanged | $0.062 – $0.125 | None |
| Effort max instead of ~70 | p × ~1.05–1.12, output tokens × ~1.6–2.5 | roughly 1.4–2× worse per kept | Some new tasks; sessions 1.6–1.8× longer for students to train on |
| Auto-generated context file | p slightly down, cost +20 % | ~1.2–1.3× worse | Down |
| 16 attempts instead of 4 | p unchanged on solved tasks | Same per kept session; cost per *newly covered* task rises as 1/p_i | Up 1.2–2.1× (from k = 1) |
| Privileged hints (patch or failing-test names) during generation, stripped before training | Raises p on hard tasks; sessions must not leak the hint | About the same | Up on the hard tail; P2T's student +10.8 |

- **Self-verification matters less here than in the test-time scaling literature**, because the hidden tests already do the selection. Its value to a generator is in cutting cost (SWE-Replay's −17 %) and in hardening graders against false positives.

### Gaps
- No Elixir measurement of any of these levers. The harness and effort numbers are Python- and terminal-centred.
- Token use per scaffold is not reported in DeepSeek's table, so harness rows cannot be turned into cost.
- Separate success figures for LLM-generated and developer-written context files are not in the abstract. Secondary write-ups give about +4 points at up to +19 % cost for developer-written files, which I could not verify against the paper.
- How much stripped hints leak into the stored sessions, and whether hint-conditioned sessions teach worse habits, is unmeasured for agents.

## 3. A poor man's teacher improvement: expert iteration and rejection-sampling fine-tuning on the teacher's own verified sessions

### Takeaway
Fine-tuning on a model's own test-passing agent sessions works when the model is weak, or poorly matched to the harness: +9.1 pass@1 and +12 pass@10 for a 72B agent in one round. It saturates after one or two rounds and can go backwards: in one setup it fell from 15.3 to 8.7, and in another it went 19.0 → 19.7 → 19.7. In math, ReST-EM gives about +6 points, with gains at every k but saturation after about two iterations. A LoRA round on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 costs roughly $0.4–2.2k on Tinker, about 10–40× less than a scaled-down RL run (my arithmetic). Its expected gain on an already strong, already RL'd teacher is small and unmeasured, and serving the tuned teacher costs about 3.6× more per attempt than the base API. So it is plausible as an Elixir-adaptation experiment, not as a way to raise yield cheaply.

### Cited Findings
- **Measured (agentic, large model, own trajectories):** Qwen2.5-72B ran each of 7,249 filtered SWE-rebench tasks 10 times and kept only trajectories whose patches passed the tests: 6,548 successes, with a one-shot demonstration used only during collection. One epoch, 50 gradient steps at 65k context, raised SWE-bench Verified pass@1 from 11.4 to 20.5 (+9.1) and pass@10 from 31.0 to 43.0 (+12.0). On SWE-rebench May pass@1 went 14.5 → 22.5 and pass@10 40.0 → 45.0 — [arXiv 2508.03501](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501v2).
- **Measured (agentic, self-improvement failure):** SWE-Gym's fine-tuned 32B OpenHands agent sampled 6 trajectories per instance (868 successes). Retraining on those plus 491 teacher trajectories *dropped* SWE-bench Lite from 15.3 % to 8.7 %; the authors say self-improvement is "not yet working" there. In MoatlessTools, two iterations of 30 rollouts per task at temperature 1.0 gave 7B 7.0 → 9.0 → 10.0 and 32B 19.0 → 19.7 → 19.7 — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2).
- **Measured (math and code, expert iteration):** ReST-EM on PaLM 2 sampled 32 solutions per MATH problem and 64 per APPS problem, kept at most 10 correct ones per problem, and always fine-tuned from the base. Gains: MATH +5.94 (S) and +6.34 (L); APPS +5.6 (S*) and +6.4 (L). On MATH, PaLM 2-L reached 41.0 % after iteration 2 and 41.9 % after iteration 3; one iteration with 3× the data reached 40.3 %. APPS gains came almost all in iteration 1, and later iterations regressed on APPS and HumanEval through overfitting on a small problem set. The tuned model beat the base at every k, most at k = 1 — [arXiv 2312.06585](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06585v1).
- **Measured:** in rejection-sampling fine-tuning, the number of distinct correct reasoning paths is the key factor. It grows by sampling more or by pooling samples from several models, and RFT helps weaker models more. Pooling raised LLaMA-7B to 49.3 % on GSM8K against 35.9 % for plain SFT — [arXiv 2308.01825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01825).
- **Measured (2026, iterative self-training plus RL):** a category-aware recipe alternates long-horizon RL with a Refresh-Repair-Expand cycle that fine-tunes on the policy's own verified successes, using no external model, then distils three 27B experts into one model. It beats its base by 4.57 points on SWE-bench Pro V2 (86.19 %) and 3.89 on SWE-bench Multilingual (71.78 %) — [arXiv 2609.23377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23377). The abstract does not give per-round gains.
- **Measured:** Step Rejection Fine-Tuning keeps failed trajectories but masks the loss on bad steps with a critic. On SWE-bench Verified, standard RFT added 2.4 points and SRFT 3.7, reaching 32.2 %, with a stronger teacher's trajectories — [arXiv 2605.10674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10674).
- **Measured:** keeping one trajectory per SWE-Gym instance (294 of 491) performed about the same as keeping all of them, until deduplication cut the data volume too far — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2).
- **Claimed:** Tinker lists DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 (552B, 16B active, 256K context) for LoRA training and lets saved checkpoints be downloaded through an API — [Tinker](https://thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/).

### Inferences
- **Where RFT gains came from:** the +9.1 for the 72B agent came from a base that was weak in the harness (11.4 %), so much of it is likely format and harness adaptation. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash is already tuned across many scaffolds. Its remaining room is Elixir knowledge and Elixir-specific recovery from `mix` errors. RFT can only re-weight what the teacher already produces, so it can teach *consistency* on Elixir idioms the teacher sometimes gets right but not idioms it never produces. I expect a gain of a few points on mined Elixir tasks, and at most about one round before saturation (by analogy with 32B 19.0 → 19.7 → 19.7 and ReST-EM on APPS). This is an estimate, not a forecast.
- **The trap:** a teacher fine-tuned on its own verified sessions produces data the student could have got from those sessions directly. Improving the teacher only adds something if it raises *coverage* of tasks the base never solves, or makes sessions shorter or cleaner. Training the student on the verified sessions directly gets most of the same value without touching the teacher. That is the cheapest form of "expert iteration": run it on the student, not the teacher.
- **Cost of one LoRA RFT round on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash** (my arithmetic, Tinker prices dated 2026-10-19):
  - Sampling: 2,000 kept sessions at p = 0.30 need ~6,700 attempts. On DeepSeek's own API that is 6,700 × $0.022–0.086 = **$150–580**.
  - Training: 2,000 sessions × 30k–80k tokens = 60M–160M train tokens × $4.98/M = **$300–800 per epoch** on Tinker. Fireworks' managed LoRA SFT for models over 300B, at $10/M, gives $600–1,600.
  - **One round ≈ $450–2,200; two rounds ≈ $0.9–4.4k.** Electricity at home is not an option for training: a 552B LoRA does not fit five 24 GB cards (**Inference**; the architecture researcher covers memory).
- **Using the tuned teacher afterwards** is the real cost. It cannot be served by DeepSeek's API. On Tinker, sampling costs $0.161 per short attempt, against $0.045 (peak) or $0.022 (off-peak) for the base on DeepSeek's API (my arithmetic, section 4): **3.6–7.3× more per attempt**. To break even on cost per kept session against the peak API price, the tuned teacher would need p ≥ 0.30 × 0.161 / 0.045 = 1.07 (my arithmetic), which is impossible. Running it at home needs dsx to load merged weights in its T16 format, which I could not confirm.

### Gaps
- No published RFT or expert-iteration result on a model of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's strength in agentic settings. The 72B evidence is from a weak base.
- Whether dsx can convert or load a LoRA-merged DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and how long merging a 552B MoE plus its Engram table takes.
- Whether Tinker's LoRA reaches the routed experts or only attention and shared layers on this model; its page does not say.
- Elixir-specific RFT gains: none published in any loop.

## 5. Home throughput for data generation: kept sessions per hour

### Takeaway
DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash through dsx is one stream at 32–41 tok/s. On mined Elixir tasks that is about **4–8 attempts per hour, or 1–2.4 kept sessions per hour at p = 0.30**, so 2,000 kept sessions would take 35–72 days of the whole box (my arithmetic). This revises the earlier report's 5–10 kept per hour down. A 30–40B-A3B teacher in vLLM was **measured at 62 sessions per hour on two 3090s and 129 per hour on all seven GPUs**, or about 19–39 kept per hour at p = 0.30. A P106 rig runs one Flint stream at a measured 44.6–45.9 tok/s, about 2–3 kept per hour per rig (est.). The rigs cannot hold the 552B teacher, so their better role is serving the student for evaluation and pass@k profiling.

### Cited Findings
- **Claimed (dsx README):** DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 ships as one 252 GB T16 GGUF. The reference machine is 2 × RTX 3090, an i7-12700K, 128 GB RAM and ~255 GB of free NVMe. It serves OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible endpoints and has a Pi setup guide — [dsx](https://github.com/lxe/dsx).
- **Measured (project, public):** in one long run, the 7-GPU machine produced 1,177 gated sessions in 9.15 h across all seven GPUs. vLLM NVFP4 Flint on two 3090s made 567 of them (62 sessions/h). Long-context decode is likely slower than the 41 tok/s short-prompt figure (the V100 path halves at 123K), so the low end is more likely.

## 6. Break-even: improving the teacher against sampling more against renting a stronger open teacher's API, and the terms on output use

### Takeaway
On cost alone, improving the teacher never pays at the planned scale of 2,000–10,000 kept sessions. DeepSeek's own API serves the base teacher at **$0.02–0.09 per attempt** and explicitly allows training other models on its outputs, including distillation. Raising p from 0.30 to 0.45 saves only **$0.02–0.10 per kept session**, so even a $6k LoRA RL run needs 60,000–250,000 kept sessions to pay back. The tuned teacher also cannot be served by that API: on Tinker it costs 3.6–7.3× more per attempt. The only case for improving the teacher is coverage of an Elixir tail the base never solves. Even there, sampling a task at p_i = 0.02 until its first pass (capped at 50 attempts) costs about $1.1–4.3 per newly covered task (my arithmetic). Use the free levers first (harness, effort, workflow prompts, hints, more attempts on hard tasks). Fine-tune the *student* on the verified sessions. Treat a teacher RFT round (~$0.5–2k) as an experiment on tasks that stay unsolved at k ≈ 50.

### Cited Findings
- **Claimed (DeepSeek API price page, read 2026-11-03):** `deepseek-flash` is served by DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017. Per 1M tokens, off-peak / peak: cache hit $0.003 / $0.006, cache miss $0.15 / $0.30, output $0.60 / $1.20. `deepseek-v4-pro` (V4-Pro-0813): $0.022 / $0.044, $0.66 / $1.32, $1.98 / $3.96. Context is 1M and max output 384K. Peak hours are 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC on weekdays, excluding Chinese public holidays, and off-peak is half price. ### Inferences
- **Per-attempt cost on DeepSeek's API (my arithmetic):**
  - Short attempt at peak: 60k × $0.30/M + 440k × $0.006/M + 20k × $1.20/M = $0.018 + $0.0026 + $0.024 = **$0.045**. Off-peak: **$0.022**.
  - Long attempt at peak: 100k × 0.30 + 1.4M × 0.006 + 40k × 1.20 = $0.030 + $0.0084 + $0.048 = **$0.086**. Off-peak: **$0.043**.
  - Peak covers only 7 hours on weekdays, so bulk generation can run almost entirely at off-peak prices.
- **Cost of the dataset by sampling alone (my arithmetic):** at p = 0.30, 2,000 kept sessions cost 6,700 × $0.022–0.086 = **$150–580**, and 10,000 kept cost **$740–2,900**. For comparison, the home 30B-class pipeline needs about 52 hours for the same 6,700 attempts, at roughly €25 of electricity, but from a weaker teacher.
- **Fixed-cost break-even (my arithmetic):** savings per kept session from raising p = c × (1/p0 − 1/p1). For 0.30 → 0.45 that is c × 1.11 = **$0.024–0.096**.

| Improvement route | One-off cost | Kept sessions to break even on API sampling savings | Serving after |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Harness, effort, prompts, hints | ~$0 to tens of dollars | Immediate | Same API, same price |
| LoRA RFT, 1–2 rounds (Tinker) | $0.45–4.4k | 4,700–180,000, **only if** the tuned teacher is served at base prices | No base-price serving: Tinker $0.161 per attempt (3.6–7.3×), a dedicated GPU deployment, or dsx if it can load the weights (unknown) |
| LoRA RL, 100 steps (Tinker) | $5.9–21.5k | 61,000–900,000, same condition | Same |
| Full RL at FireFlash scale (rented) | $34–73k+ | 350,000–3,000,000, same condition | Self-hosted cluster or dedicated endpoint |

- **Break-even with hosting costs (my arithmetic):** a tuned teacher on Tinker costs $0.161 per short attempt. At p1 = 0.45 that is $0.358 per kept session, against $0.074 (off-peak) to $0.149 (peak) for the base on the API, so the improved teacher is **2.4–4.9× more expensive per kept session**. It breaks even only above p1 = 0.30 × 0.161 / 0.045 = 1.07, which is impossible. **Improving the teacher therefore cannot win on yield economics unless it can run at home or at base API prices.** At home its only gain is box-time: 1.5× more kept per hour at 1–4 kept/h.
- **Coverage, the one real case (my arithmetic):** a task with per-attempt pass rate p_i needs 1/p_i attempts on average for a first pass. At p_i = 0.02, a cap of 50 attempts covers 1 − 0.98⁵⁰ = 64 % of such tasks. If sampling stops at the first pass, the expected attempts per task are (1 − 0.98⁵⁰) / 0.02 = 31.8. The cost per newly covered task is then c / p_i = 50 × $0.022–0.086 = **$1.1–4.3** on the API. 1,000 such tasks cost about $0.7–2.7k and yield ~640 newly covered tasks, the same order as an RFT round before its serving cost. For tasks at p_i = 0, more samples never help. FireFlash found about half of those were broken, so the first step is to audit them, not to train. RFT on the teacher's own successes cannot reach them either, because it has no successes there. That leaves privileged hints (P2T-style), Elixir docs in the prompt, or a stronger teacher.
- **Renting a stronger open teacher:**
  - DeepSeek V4-Pro on the same API costs per short attempt 60k × $1.32/M + 440k × $0.044/M + 20k × $3.96/M = $0.079 + $0.019 + $0.079 = **$0.18 peak / $0.09 off-peak**, about 4× Flash (my arithmetic). It is covered by the same Open Platform terms.
  - It is worth a 50-task trial on Flash's unsolved tail before any teacher training: if Pro covers that tail at k ≤ 8, it costs about $1 per newly covered task (8 × $0.09 off-peak + misses) with no training.
  - Its agentic and Elixir scores were not read here (gap). Tinker also lists GLM-5.3, Kimi-K2.6 and Qwen3.5-397B for sampling, at $2.11–7.50 per 1M sampled tokens, but their licences and output terms were not checked.
- **Which hosts fit the data rule:** DeepSeek's own API does, explicitly (§4.2). Local weights under MIT do. Fireworks is ambiguous because §2.2(c) conflicts with §3.2. Under a rule that every byte must trace to an approved source, avoid Fireworks-generated sessions unless Fireworks confirms in writing. This is a reading of the terms, not legal advice.
- **Order of work this implies:**
  1. Run the base on DeepSeek's API (off-peak) inside the student's deployment harness at effort ~60–80, with workflow prompts and up to 8–16 attempts per task.
  2. Spend extra attempts only on tasks still unsolved at k = 8, up to ~50.
  3. Audit tasks that are never solved; try hints, the Pro model or docs on the survivors.
  4. Train the student on the verified sessions.
  5. Only then try one teacher RFT round, measured on coverage at k = 16 over held-out mined tasks, never pass@1.

- Tinker's terms of service on output use, and whether a Tinker LoRA checkpoint for V4.1-Flash can be served anywhere at near-base prices, were not checked.
